Dialogue and character diagnosis
Distinguish plot logic from human interaction: a logically correct scene can still read like people executing a story function. Examine exchanges in context, including actions, evasions, interpretation and consequences; do not diagnose from punctuation, sentence lengths, pronouns or turn counts.
- FUNCTIONAL_DIALOGUE concerns lines reduced to delivering information, arranging actions or explaining the situation at the expense of plausible personal motives. Brief practical talk that suits the scene is not a defect.
- OVER_STRUCTURED_DIALOGUE concerns an overly complete conversational protocol: a proposition receives an exact objection, then clarification, a precisely targeted question, and a fully articulated answer. Even emotional lines can efficiently advance one shared argument. Look for a sustained pattern in which nearly every response neatly answers the last line or services the same decision/requirement, compressing the participants' own reactions. Evasion, misunderstanding, unfinished expression, emotional drift, interruption and subtext are possible counter-signals, not mandatory devices. Absence of silence or interruption alone proves nothing. A formal discussion, clear reasoning or purposeful procedural exchange may work well; explain the specific human/reading effect before reporting a problem.
- CHARACTERS_AS_ARGUMENTS needs strong evidence that participants primarily embody abstract positions or solutions, with differences supplied by their opinions rather than personality, immediate motives, relationship, pride, fear, bias or experience. Disagreement, a focused conflict and two reasonable positions are not triggers. Observe how the specific people react and what they risk; do not demand invented backstory or a new canon fact.
- WEAK_CHARACTER_VOICE concerns insufficient differentiation in rhythm, syntax, avoidance, emotional expression, judgment and response habits. Two opposing speakers may still share the same polished summarizing, questioning and rebuttal habits and the same unusually complete access to their own psychological logic. Merely counting who speaks more is insufficient. Formal, calm, restrained or literary voices can be distinctive. Do not prescribe slang, catchphrases, filler words or short sentences as a universal cure. Do not claim out-of-character behavior without supplied character evidence.
Use character specificity as a supporting lens: could different names replace these people without changing their interaction? What supplied relational detail, individual interpretation or reaction makes this encounter specific? Weak relationship-specific detail may support CHARACTERS_AS_ARGUMENTS or WEAK_CHARACTER_VOICE in description/evidence/impact; it is not a new mandatory code and not a demand for biography. Ground the judgment in the actual exchange and acknowledge contrary evidence.
